## HCD.9:5 - Archetypal Grounding {#archetypal-grounding}

### HCD.9:5.1 - Redirect a Highest-NPV Answer to the Tested Configuration {#redirect-a-highest-npv-answer-to-the-tested-configuration}

In the release case, the learner's first answer selects external module B because its stipulated NPV is highest. Ordinary references and calculation tools are permitted. The financial calculation remains useful, but the answer relies on a vendor report without checking which firmware was tested.

The teacher does not replace the answer. The feedback is one discriminating question: “Which configuration does the report support?” The learner finds that the report concerns FW7 while the required release uses FW8, marks B as unsupported for that use, preserves the financial comparison as conditional, and requests FW8-compatible evidence from the report's supplier and withholds reliance on B until that evidence is obtained and checked. This completes the local correction; comparison with other modules requires their own compatible evidence.

The retry changes the condition. A compatible HW3/FW8/SCHED2 report and 24 test hours now support ten changes in three homogeneous batches, which require 23 hours, while demand of twelve leaves a continuing queue. Repeating the earlier refusal is wrong. If the available time is then 22 hours, promising ten changes becomes unsupported. The episode shows whether the learner changed configuration and resource checking under the recorded support; it does not yet establish unfamiliar transfer or retention.

### HCD.9:5.2 - Turn a Pump Claim into a Discriminating Observation {#turn-a-pump-claim-into-a-discriminating-observation}

Use the public pump task in HCD.6:5.1, including its 20 kW/100 m³/h baseline, measurement uncertainty, comparison model and protected observation time. The learner's attempt uses 22.3 kW and 99.5 m³/h and concludes that increased energy per volume proves higher resistance. The calculation and supplied uncertainty bounds support the increase and weaken the measurement-error explanation; resistance and regulation efficiency remain rivals.

Feedback preserves the calculation and asks which feasible pressure observation would distinguish the leading explanations. The learner revises the claim, requests the comparable pressure observation within the remaining one and a half specialist-hours supplied by HCD.6:5.1, and states how a value near 448 kPa would support increased resistance under the stipulated model while a value near 400 kPa would redirect attention toward regulation efficiency. The correction is the conditional explanation and observation choice, not a teacher-supplied cause.

A later case changes uncertainty or omits comparable pressure data. If no obtainable measurement can improve the current explanation or response enough to justify its full preparation, specialist, comparison and displaced-work burden, the learner can finish with the supported energy-per-volume change and qualified rival explanations. A feasible present response is to return that bounded account to the responsible practitioner while leaving a resistance-specific remedy unselected. The cause remains unresolved; the useful calculation and explanation do not become incomplete merely because no new measurement is commissioned. Under the stated practice criteria, this can be a successful corrected retry.

When the changed condition instead makes the pressure observation useful enough to warrant its obtainable cost within the real window, select it and state how its possible values change the response. Keep the observation result unknown until it is obtained. Neither branch permits a unique-cause claim from the energy calculation alone; actual operating protections retain their own conditions and authority.

### HCD.9:5.3 - Fit a Targeted Retry into Three Hours {#fit-a-targeted-retry-into-three-hours}

The programme's retry slot allocates 135 minutes to the learner's correction and short changed case, 15 minutes to an individual teacher check, and 30 minutes to recording the result: three hours in total. The teacher chooses a case small enough for the affected requirement to be checked in that interval.

Requirements already demonstrated remain supported unless the new response contradicts them. If several independent gaps cannot be corrected and checked in the slot, the remaining requirements are recorded as incomplete. Continuing requires a separate decision about time or a narrower output; it is not added silently to the 340-hour programme and overall group success does not erase it.

### HCD.9:5.4 - Keep Demonstration and AI Help out of the First Attempt {#keep-demonstration-and-ai-help-out-of-the-first-attempt}

A teacher may demonstrate one move on an anchor case before the learner receives a distinct variant. The answer and key remain with the teacher; on the variant, the learner first receives the condition. If a detailed cue or demonstration is supplied after difficulty, the resulting correction is supported and the trace says so.

AI may search, calculate, draft, or critique within the permitted boundary. The trace records the learner's question before AI use, the generated contribution, decisive source and calculation checks, and the learner's explanation. A fluent AI revision is not the learner's correction until the learner checks and uses it. A later changed-support probe may be selected by HCD.10, while HCD.11–HCD.13 govern what that observation can establish.

## HCD.9:6 - Bias-Annotation {#bias-annotation}

Lenses: **Gov**, **Arch**, **Onto/Epist**, **Prag**, **Did**. Scope: one focused practice–feedback–correction–retry episode for a named human action.

| Recurring bias | Likely drift | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hours-dose bias | Logged time is treated as the mechanism or proof of improvement. | Preserve the task, action, support, feedback, correction, and retry. |
| corrected-product bias | The final answer overwrites the first attempt and received help. | Keep first response, provider contribution, learner correction, and retry separate. |
| answer-substitution bias | Teacher, peer, or AI output becomes the learner's performance. | Require a learner action after the contribution and retain attribution. |
| score-only bias | A mark or threshold supplies no actionable information. | Name the criterion, observed error, useful part, and next action. |
| feedback-immediacy bias | One timing rule is applied regardless of risk and purpose. | Choose timing from safety, consequence, first-attempt value, and error rehearsal. |
| exact-repeat bias | Memorizing the corrected answer is mistaken for rule use. | Change a decision-bearing condition when discrimination or adaptation matters. |
| total-retest bias | One defect causes every supported requirement to be repeated. | Retry affected requirements and reopen others only when contradicted or material. |
| practice-effect bias | A local changed attempt becomes capability, transfer, retention, or programme-effect proof. | Limit the conclusion and route stronger inference to HCD.11–HCD.13. |

## HCD.9:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them {#common-anti-patterns-and-how-to-avoid-them}

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Practise for ten more hours” | Duration does not identify the action, error, feedback, or observation. | Select one practice focus and close one traceable loop. |
| “Feedback was provided” | Content, recipient, timing, and opportunity to use it are unknown. | Record the exact criterion-bearing contribution and next action. |
| “Here is the correct answer” | Provider output can replace the learner action. | Use the smallest adequate cue and obtain a learner-authored correction. |
| “The corrected file proves mastery” | The first attempt and support have disappeared. | Preserve attempt, help, correction, and retry as different facts. |
| “Repeat the same question” | Answer memory can mimic the intended discrimination. | Change the condition that should change the warranted move. |
| “Retake the whole module” | Already supported requirements and the exact gap are lost. | Retry the affected requirement and preserve compatible success. |
| “They improved after feedback” | Temporal order does not establish causality or durable capability. | Report changed performance under the recorded conditions and seek stronger evidence separately. |
| “Everyone passed overall” | A critical or individual unresolved gap is hidden by aggregation. | Keep holder-level requirements, safe stops, and continuations visible. |

## HCD.9:10 - Rationale {#rationale}

Feedback contributes to development only through what reaches a learner and can be used in another action. Its name, score, or provider intention cannot substitute for content, timing, receipt, correction, and retry. Preserving the first attempt protects both learning design and later evidence interpretation.

A local correction is valuable without proving a general capability. The episode result therefore ends at changed or unchanged action under stated conditions and sends transfer, retention, causal, and consequential decisions to the Methods that can support them.

## HCD.9:11 - SoTA-Echoing {#sota-echoing}

The working question is: **How should a practitioner conduct a focused practice attempt and feedback loop that produces a usable correction without losing attribution or overclaiming the result?**

The selected best-known line treats feedback as task- and information-specific and connects it to an opportunity for correction and retest. Wisniewski, Zierer, and Hattie's 2020 meta-analysis ([`MOT-05`](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087)) shows substantial heterogeneity across feedback forms and outcomes; task, information content, receiver, and opportunity to use it are material, while the mean effect does not select praise, score, corrective information, timing, or channel for this case. Winget and Persky's 2022 practical review ([`COG-06`](https://doi.org/10.5688/ajpe8906)) supports formative assessment, corrective activity, retesting, and an explicit threshold as one coherent arrangement, but not one universal threshold or later professional-performance claim. HCD.9 adapts these contributions into §§4.2–4.6.

The serious ordinary alternative is dose-and-score practice: assign more hours, provide a grade or model answer, keep the final submission, and infer improvement from completion. It is inexpensive to administer, but it loses the first attempt, actual help, information-bearing feedback, learner correction, changed-condition discrimination, and unresolved requirements. HCD.9 retains time and scores as episode conditions while rejecting them as the practice mechanism or sufficient result.

Nurse and colleagues' 2024 systematic review of deliberate practice for therapeutic skills ([`BEH-03`](https://doi.org/10.1080/10503307.2024.2308159)) supports focused objectives, guidance, feedback, and repeated refinement as promising for discrete therapeutic skills while leaving the evidence base, delivery form, patient outcome, broad professional capability, and durable transfer unsettled. Macnamara, Hambrick, and Oswald's 2014 meta-analysis ([`BEH-02`](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810)) shows that retrospective deliberate-practice histories explain differing and incomplete shares of performance variance across domains; it does not establish a universal hours threshold, causality, or cross-domain transfer. These limits produce the episode-level result and explicit non-dose boundary.

The selected line costs more observation and traceability than counting hours and completions, but it preserves learner attribution, actionable information, critical errors, usable correction, and the exact next focus. Reopen it when the target action, task, support, risk, feedback source or content, retry condition, population evidence, or serious alternative changes, or when repeated use shows that a lighter trace preserves the same decision-bearing distinctions.
